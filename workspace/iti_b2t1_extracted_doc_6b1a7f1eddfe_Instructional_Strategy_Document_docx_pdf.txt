 
Instructional Strategy Document (ISD) for Project on AI-Readiness for Trades 
Course contents - LMS Self-Study Package + Instructor Use Guide 
1.​ Purpose and Intended Use of this ISD 
This document defines the instructional strategy and build rules for creating: 
●​ the self-study LMS package (≈20 hours) for ITI learners in < trade name >, and 
●​ the Instructor Use Guide that enables classroom and lab instructors to help learners apply what 
they learned on LMS during regular teaching and practical sessions. 
It ensures all IDs, writers, SMEs, and reviewers develop assets with a shared understanding of: 
●​ what the course must achieve; 
●​ what to include and what to avoid; 
●​ how to structure and package content consistently for LMS; and 
●​ how the instructor will integrate AI readiness into classroom and lab sessions. 
 
2.​ Learner, Context, and Design Implications 
●​ Learners: ITI learners; non-native English speakers; first time on AI-readiness content; learning 
alongside NCVET trade curriculum. 
●​ LMS context: self-study only; no simulation/practice on LMS; engagement must be maintained 
through short, varied micro learning units in each topic. 
●​ Instructor context: one classroom/lab instructor who teaches trade and runs practical sessions; 
will integrate AI usage into regular course delivery through the guidelines provided in the 
Instructor Use Guide. 
Implications: 
●​ LMS content must stay at Knowledge + Comprehension only (Bloom’s taxonomy). 
●​ Language and structure must be simple, predictable, repeatable. 
●​ Practical application must be driven through the Instructor Use Guide, aligned to 18–20 selected 
NCVET practical in the specific trades 
 
3.​ Program Goal and Scope Boundary 
Goal: Prepare learners to recognise AI-enabled/digital support in trade learning and workplace, 
understand common outputs/alerts, and build “trust vs verify” awareness so that they become 
confident in using AI in class/lab and at work. 
LMS scope: awareness + comprehension + safe-use thinking (no hands-on execution). 
Instructor guide scope: practical integration and class/lab facilitation (how to practice, what prompts 
to use, what to observe, what evidence to capture etc). 
4.​ Anchoring to NCVET trades 
All content must map back to trade realities for the trades. For example for electrical and wiremen 
trade: wiring/installation, testing & measurement, protection devices, earthing, safe isolation, basic 
fault identification logic, and documentation/job notes etc.

9.​ Instructor Use Guide (Classroom + Lab Integration) — Strategy & Build Brief 
9.1 Purpose 
The Instructor Use Guide enables trade instructors to make learners practice the concepts learned 
through the Self-study mode during everyday classroom and lab sessions, without creating a 
separate “AI class.” It provides: 
●​ where and how to reference LMS learning during teaching, and 
●​ a structured method to integrate AI into 18–20 selected NCVET practicals. 
9.2 Instructor approach (how AI is integrated) 
The instructor will use two modes: 
A) Classroom integration (during theory or brief class moments) 
●​ 5–10 minute “AI-linked” prompts during relevant lessons 
●​ quick discussion questions aligned to LMS topics 
●​ small check-for-understanding moments (oral / show-of-hands / quick written) 
B) Lab integration (aligned to selected practical sessions) 
For each selected practical, instructor will guide learners to use AI as a support tool, e.g.: 
●​ generating a step checklist before starting 
●​ listing tools/materials needed 
●​ predicting common mistakes 
●​ interpreting what a reading/output could indicate (conceptual) 
●​ writing a simple job note after completion 
Important boundary: AI is used to support learning and reasoning, while the instructor ensures all 
work follows safety and trade procedures.

9.4 “AI Add-on Card” template (one page per practical) 
Each selected practical must include: 
●​ Practical Name (NCVET wording) 
●​ What learners already do in this practical (1–2 lines) 
●​ Where AI can help learning (before / during / after) 
●​ Suggested AI prompts (3–5 prompts, in simple English) 
●​ Instructor questions (3 questions) to check understanding 
●​ Safety & verification reminder (2–3 bullets) 
●​ Space for expected learner output (non-submission) 
o​
e.g., checklist, tool list, “common mistakes” list, 4-line job note 
●​ Evidence the instructor can capture quickly (tick/notes) 
o​
e.g., “learner produced checklist” / “learner explained reading meaning”

10.​Quality Gate (for LMS + Instructor Guide)

●​ LMS checks 
●​ every topic has the full asset recipe

●​ sequencing and naming conventions followed 
●​ Instructor guide checks 
●​ at least 18–20 practicals with AI Add-on Cards included 
●​ prompts are simple and safe 
●​ clear mapping from LMS modules to class/lab usage 
●​ evidence capture method is lightweight and realistic
