# LinkedIn post, 2026-05-20

Today’s useful AI lesson from my own workspace:

Sometimes the best agent output is not “more content.”

It is a control sheet that stops you from doing random work.

I had a digital product backlog for an Etsy experiment:

• a shop trust kit
• a first product upload packet
• a few verified ZIP products
• listing copy, images, tags, and SOP notes

The easy AI move would have been:
“Make another product.”

The better move was:
“Tell me what is actually blocking the revenue loop.”

The answer was boring and useful:

Do not create another SKU today.
Finish the shop trust setup.
Upload the first product packet.
Verify the files are attached.
Save/publish only after the preview is correct.

That turned into a simple publishing control sheet:

1. What to upload first
2. Which product to prioritize
3. Exact title, tags, and description
4. Which ZIP/images are verified
5. What must be checked before publishing

This is where AI agents are becoming practical for me.

Not just ideation.
Not just writing.
Not just “build faster.”

More like:

Read the messy workspace.
Find the bottleneck.
Reduce the next action to something I can do in 30 minutes.
Keep me from creating more unfinished inventory.

That last part matters.

A lot of builders do not need more ideas.
They need a system that points at the unfinished loop and says:

“This is the next bottleneck. Clear this before adding more.”

That is the kind of AI workflow I want to keep building into Hermes, Wise AI, and my own projects.

Question: what is one project where you do not need more ideas, you need a control sheet for the next real action?
