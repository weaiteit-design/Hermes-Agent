# LinkedIn post, 2026-05-15

Most people judge AI tools by the model.

But in real workflows, I am seeing something else matter just as much:

the harness around the model.

A good AI workflow is rarely just:
“ask ChatGPT and hope.”

It is more like:

1. Give the model the right context
2. Define what “good output” looks like
3. Let it use the right tools
4. Add a small verification step
5. Save the repeatable process

This applies everywhere:

• coding agents need tests, repo maps, and guardrails
• research agents need source notes and citation checks
• content agents need voice rules and examples
• creative AI workflows need briefs, references, and review loops
• business workflows need clear inputs, owner approval, and logs

The model is the engine.

The workflow is the vehicle.

I think the next big productivity jump will not come from everyone chasing 100 new tools.

It will come from turning 5 repeated tasks into reliable AI-assisted systems.

Small example:
If you write content every day, don’t just ask for “a LinkedIn post.”
Create a simple routine:

trend scan → angle selection → draft → voice check → risk check → copy-paste pack → save learnings

That is boring.

But boring systems compound.

Question for builders/operators here:
What is one repeated task in your week that deserves a proper AI workflow, not just a prompt?
