# LinkedIn post, 2026-05-17

AI in education is getting more attention right now.

Not just as a tool for answers, but as a way to create better learning loops.

That distinction matters.

A weak use of AI looks like:

"Explain this topic to me."

A stronger learning workflow looks like:

1. Explain the concept simply
2. Give me 3 examples
3. Ask me 5 questions
4. Find where I am confused
5. Give me a smaller exercise
6. Make me explain it back
7. Turn my mistakes into revision notes

The value is not that AI gives an instant explanation.

The real value is that it can keep adjusting the next step based on where you are stuck.

This is useful for students, but also for builders and operators.

You can use the same loop to learn:

• a new AI tool
• a coding concept
• a market or industry
• a design style
• a sales or copywriting framework
• a workflow you want to automate

I think the next skill is not just "using AI."

It is learning how to build feedback loops with AI:

try → get feedback → correct → retry → package the lesson

Access to AI tools will keep getting easier.

But the people who benefit most will be the ones who turn that access into deliberate practice.

Small reminder to myself too:

Don't just ask AI for the answer.
Use it to build the loop that makes you better.

Question: what is one skill you would learn faster if AI acted like a patient tutor instead of just an answer machine?
