# LinkedIn post, 2026-05-19

A pattern I am seeing in AI agents right now:

The model is not the whole product.

The real value often comes from the system around it:

• What triggers the agent?
• What context does it receive?
• Where is it allowed to act?
• How is the work sandboxed?
• What does the human review before anything ships?

This matters because “AI agent” can sound like magic.

But in daily work, useful agents are usually quite practical.

Example:

Weak setup:
“Agent, help with my project.”

Better setup:
“Every morning, check the project notes, compare yesterday’s plan with what changed, flag blockers, draft 3 next actions, and wait for approval before making changes.”

That is not just a prompt.
It is a workflow.

I am starting to think of agents in 5 parts:

1. Trigger
When should it run?

2. Context
What files, notes, tools, or sources should it read?

3. Workspace
Where can it safely experiment?

4. Output
What artifact should it produce?

5. Review
What must a human approve?

This framing makes AI feel less vague.

For students, builders, creators, and small teams, the opportunity is not only to use better models.

It is to turn repeated messy work into small, reviewable systems.

A research brief.
A product draft.
A content pack.
A bug report.
A customer follow-up.
A weekly learning plan.

The more I use AI tools, the more I realize the boring structure around the model is where a lot of the leverage lives.

Question for builders: what recurring task in your week deserves a trigger, context, workspace, output, and review loop?
