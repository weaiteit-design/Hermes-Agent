# LinkedIn post, 2026-05-18

A useful pattern I am noticing in AI workflows:

The best outputs are not “done for you.”
They are “ready for review.”

That sounds small, but it changes how you use AI.

A weak workflow:

“Make me a strategy doc.”

A stronger workflow:

“Read these notes, find the messy parts, separate facts from assumptions, list the missing inputs, then draft a decision memo I can review.”

The difference is accountability.

AI can move fast through scattered context:

• project notes
• dashboards
• customer feedback
• meeting summaries
• docs and spreadsheets
• old decisions
• rough ideas

But the human still needs to own the judgment:

• Is the source reliable?
• What assumptions are hidden?
• What is actually proven?
• What needs one more check?
• What decision are we making next?

This is why I like thinking of AI as a first-draft operations partner, not an autopilot.

For builders, students, and small teams, the opportunity is simple:

Turn repeated messy work into reviewable artifacts.

Not just prompts.
Actual workflows.

Example:

Input: scattered notes from a project
AI task: summarize changes, flag blockers, list open decisions, draft next actions
Human task: verify, choose, edit, ship

That loop is more valuable than asking for a generic answer.

Small reminder to myself too:

Don’t use AI only to finish tasks faster.
Use it to make your thinking easier to inspect.

Question: what is one messy recurring task in your week that could become a reviewable AI workflow?
