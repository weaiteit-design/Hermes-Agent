# LinkedIn post, 2026-05-16

A small shift I am trying to make with AI:

Stop asking for outputs.
Start designing briefs.

The difference looks tiny, but it changes the quality a lot.

A weak prompt says:
"Make me an ad for this product."

A better brief says:

1. Who is this for?
2. What problem are they already feeling?
3. What should they believe after seeing it?
4. What proof or reference should guide the style?
5. What format do we need?
6. What should we avoid?
7. How will we judge if it worked?

This matters more as AI becomes part of creative work, startup experiments, and digital product building.

The tool can generate 20 options in seconds.
But if the brief is unclear, you just get 20 versions of confusion.

For a student, founder, creator, or operator, the leverage is not only in knowing which AI tool is trending.

It is in learning how to give AI a clear problem, constraints, references, and review loop.

A simple workflow I like:

brief → first draft → critique against the brief → improve → package for the channel

This works for:

• Etsy listing images
• social posts
• small business ad concepts
• landing page copy
• research summaries
• pitch/storytelling assets

The lesson I keep coming back to:

AI rewards people who can think clearly before they generate quickly.

Speed is useful.
Direction is leverage.

Question: what is one recurring creative or business task where a better brief would improve your AI output immediately?
